# 02 · Voice and writing

We sound like a thoughtful colleague who understands hospitals. The voice stays the same everywhere; the tone adjusts to the moment.

## Our voice

| Trait | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Calm | We state things simply and let the facts carry weight. |
| Warm | We talk about patients and teams, never “users” or “leads”. |
| Exact | Real numbers, real outcomes. Nothing rounded up, nothing invented. |

## Tone by situation

The voice stays the same. The tone changes with what the reader is doing.

| Situation | How it sounds | Example | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guiding | Calm, one step at a time | First, confirm your phone number. Then choose a time that suits you. | One step at a time keeps people from feeling lost. |
| Informing | Plain and factual | Your report is ready. It was added to your record on 22 Nov. | Facts first, with no medical jargon. |
| Reminding | Warm, never nagging | Your follow-up with Dr. Rao is tomorrow at 10:30. Reply 1 to confirm. | Gives the what, the when and one easy way to act. |
| Responding to a problem | Calm, accountable, helpful | We couldn’t send your request. Check your connection and try again. | Says what happened and how to fix it, without blame. |
| Confirming | Warm, brief | Request sent. Someone from our team will be in touch shortly. | Closes the loop and sets an expectation. |
| Headline | Calm, declarative | Extending attention at the heart of care. | States the idea in one line. |

## Writing principles

**Do**

- Say “we” for Cura42 and “you” for the hospital.
- Keep language simple: familiar words, short sentences.
- Use sentence case for headlines, buttons and menus.
- Use digits and sourced numbers: 12 hospitals.
- Use British/Indian spelling: organisation, colour.
- Keep headlines under 10 words.

**Don't**

- Use exclamation marks or emoji.
- Use hype: revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless.
- Round up or invent a statistic.
- Type labels in capitals; styles handle that.
- Write “Click here” or “Submit”.

## Writing about health

- Be supportive and respectful. Treat every patient as able to understand their own care.
- Don’t blame or judge: write “Let’s find a new time”, not “You missed your appointment”.
- Don’t exaggerate or promise outcomes: no “guaranteed recovery”, no “cure”.
- Don’t make medical claims the care team hasn’t approved; point to the hospital’s own guidance.
- Use the clinician’s name and the hospital’s own words for treatments and procedures.
- When something is urgent, say what to do and who to call first.

## UI writing patterns

| Element | Pattern | Write | Not | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Button | Verb + object, 2–3 words | Request a demo | Submit | Says what happens next. |
| Field label | A noun, sentence case, no colon | Phone number | Enter your phone number: | Short labels scan faster and translate better. |
| Helper text | One short sentence that helps before the mistake | We’ll only use this to confirm your visit. | This field is required for processing. | Explains why, in the person’s terms. |
| Validation message | What to do, not what failed | Enter a 10-digit phone number | Invalid input | Tells people how to fix it. |
| Empty state | What will appear, plus one action | No follow-ups yet. Discharged patients appear here. | Nothing to show | Explains the blank and gives a next step. |
| Loading message | Name what is loading | Loading your records… | Please wait | Names the thing instead of being vague. |
| Alert | The situation, then what to do | 3 follow-ups are due today. | Attention! | Factual and actionable. |
| Confirmation | Name the action and its effect | Remove patient from list? They’ll stop getting reminders. | Are you sure? | Names the consequence. |

## Word list

| Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| patients | users, leads, cases | People, not records. |
| care team | staff resources | Respectful and human. |
| AI agent | bot, robot | Honest about what it is without sounding cold. |
| follow-up | touchpoint | A plain word people already use. |
| attention | engagement | Matches what care teams give. |
| book a demo | get started now | Specific about the action. |

## Length guide

| Element | Aim for | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Button | 2–3 words | 25 characters |
| Field label | 1–3 words | 30 characters |
| Helper text | One sentence | 90 characters |
| Validation message | One sentence | 110 characters |
| Toast | Title and one line | 40 and 80 characters |
| Dialog title | A question or an action | 8 words |
| Empty state | Title and one line | 40 and 100 characters |

## Before you publish

- Accessibility: write link and button text that makes sense on its own; don’t refer to colour or position (“the red button”); give meaningful images alt text.
- Readability: use familiar words and sentences under 20 words. Read it aloud once.
- Length: stay within the length guide. Text can grow by up to 40% when translated.
- Localisation: write whole sentences instead of joining fragments; avoid idioms and puns; keep text out of images; format dates, times and numbers by locale.
