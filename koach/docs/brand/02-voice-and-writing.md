# 02 · Voice and writing

Neutral, professional and plain, like a senior colleague giving clear instructions. Warm in the learner workspace, brisk in the consoles. Never cute, never alarming.

## Our voice

| Trait | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Plain | Short sentences and familiar words, in sentence case everywhere. |
| Professional | A senior colleague giving clear instructions, not a marketer. |
| Calm | Warm for learners, brisk for admins, and never alarming. |

## Tone by situation

The voice stays the same. The tone changes with what the reader is doing.

| Situation | How it sounds | Example | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guiding | Warm, speaks to “you” | Your next steps | Speaks to the learner directly and shows what comes next. |
| Informing | Plain and factual | Your tests and feedback are in the learning journey. | Says where things are, with no extra words. |
| Reminding | Factual, never alarming | Due in 5 days | States the fact and the time without pressure. |
| Responding to a problem | Calm, no apology | We couldn’t load this session. Try again. | Says what went wrong and offers the fix. |
| Confirming | Brisk, names the object | Hospital added | Closes the loop in two words. |
| Headline | Warm and short | A small step to better care | Used on the onboarding intro screen. |

## Writing principles

**Do**

- Use sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, tabs, table headers, menu items.
- Start buttons with a verb and say what happens: “Add hospital”, “Save changes”.
- Talk to the learner as “you”; name the object in console copy: “Delete document?”.
- Write errors that say what went wrong and how to fix it.
- Keep acronyms in capitals: NABH, JCI, NABL, ISO, HOD, OJT.
- Be warm in the learner workspace and brisk in the consoles.

**Don't**

- Use emoji in product UI.
- Write “OK” for a destructive action, or “Click here”.
- Apologise in error messages.
- Use Title Case for titles, buttons or headers.
- Sound cute or alarming.
- Let colour alone carry a status; every status has a word.

## Writing for clinical learning

- Use programme names and codes as issued, for example KH-IC-220, in tabular figures.
- Write compliance as acronyms joined by commas, then “mandatory”: “NABH, JCI mandatory”.
- State overdue and due facts plainly: “2 days overdue”, “Due today”. Never scold or alarm.
- Keep clinical terms as the hospital writes them. Don’t reword a standard or a procedure name.
- Confirmations ask the question, then state the consequence.

## UI writing patterns

| Element | Pattern | Write | Not | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Button | Verb first, sentence case | Add hospital | Click here | Says exactly what happens. |
| Field label | A noun, sentence case | Work email | Enter your Work Email: | Short labels scan faster. |
| Helper text | One line that helps before the mistake | Use your hospital email address. | Required | Explains what to enter, not that it is needed. |
| Validation message | What went wrong and how to fix it | Enter a valid email address, like name@kauveryhospital.com. | Invalid email | Tells people how to fix it. No apology. |
| Empty state | Name what will appear | Sessions you register for appear here. | No data | Explains the blank and what fills it. |
| Loading message | Name what is loading | Loading your sessions… | Please wait | Names the thing instead of being vague. |
| Error | What happened, plus an action | We couldn’t load this session. | Error 500 | States the problem; the action is “Try again”. |
| Confirmation | Ask the question, then state the consequence | Delete document? Hand hygiene SOP will be removed for all learners. This cannot be undone. | Are you sure? | Names the object and what will happen. |

## Formats

| Thing | Write it as |
| --- | --- |
| Dates | 12 Nov 2026 in prose and headers; 03/11/2026 (DD/MM/YYYY) in tables and date fields; Thu, 12 Nov on compact chips |
| Times and ranges | 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM, with a spaced en dash |
| Relative due | Due in 5 days · 2 days overdue · Due today |
| Counts | 2 topics · 4 units · 12/25 remaining · Step 2 of 3 · Question 1 of 2 |
| Compliance | NABH, JCI mandatory |
| Programme codes | As issued, in tabular figures: KH-IC-220 |
| Pagination | Learner workspace: Records per page. Consoles: Rows per page |

## Word list

| Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Register now | Click here to register | Starts with a verb and says what happens. |
| Save changes | OK | Never “OK” for an action. |
| Delete document? | Are you sure? | Names the object. |
| Try again | Retry failed | Plain and direct. |
| Records per page / Rows per page | Items per page | Learner workspace says Records; consoles say Rows. |
| HOD, OJT, NABH | Hod, Ojt, Nabh | Acronyms keep their capitals. |

## Length guide

| Element | Aim for | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Button | 2–3 words | 25 characters |
| Field label | 1–3 words | 30 characters |
| Helper text | One sentence | 90 characters |
| Validation message | One sentence | 110 characters |
| Toast | Title and one line | 40 and 80 characters |
| Dialog title | A question or an action | 8 words |
| Empty state | Title and one line | 40 and 100 characters |

## Before you publish

- Accessibility: write link and button text that makes sense on its own; don’t refer to colour or position (“the red button”); give meaningful images alt text.
- Readability: use familiar words and sentences under 20 words. Read it aloud once.
- Length: stay within the length guide. Text can grow by up to 40% when translated.
- Localisation: write whole sentences instead of joining fragments; avoid idioms and puns; keep text out of images; format dates, times and numbers by locale.
